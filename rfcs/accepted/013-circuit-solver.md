The amendment also fixes the numerics the first text left to the implementing pull request or got wrong, each checked against a prototype before it was written: the local error estimator and its constants (rule 11), variable-step Gear 2 (rule 6), controlled start-up (rule 7), the Newton criteria and the junction limiter (rule 8), the initial solve (rule 9), the golden scenario `rc-step` and its bound (rule 21); and completes the profile (rule 2). A second reader checked the amended text against the code and a model of the rules; its corrections are in it: the junction limiter lives in the device (rule 8), the initial solve and its stages (rule 9), the error constant of variable-step Gear 2 (rule 11), the step limits (rule 13), the pairing and the outcomes of the comparison (rule 20) and what the kit's results file holds (rule 21). Rule numbers of the first text are kept. Companion of RFC 012, which owns the domain's types, contract and time coupling; this RFC owns the numbers: how the plant is formulated, solved, stepped and compared across Hosts.

**Tracks.** Requirements PHY-003, PHY-004, PHY-005, PHY-006, PHY-010, PHY-012, NUM-001, NUM-002, NUM-003, NUM-005, NUM-006, AUTO-004, AUTO-005, DES-006, NFR-004. Requirement IDs refer to `docs/src/requirements.md`.

## 1. What the solver promises

The solver is Host code of `florn-domain-circuit`. It advances the plant from one tick to a later one (RFC 012 rule 7) with the textbook methods of PHY-003 to PHY-005 and nothing cleverer: the point of 0.4 is a controller lab and a test of the core, not a competitor to a circuit simulator. Everything it does is a deterministic function of the run inputs and of the sequence of events and breakpoints the queue hands it (RFC 012 rules 8 to 11); the only source of difference between two Hosts is floating-point arithmetic, and section 9 closes that gap as far as the engines allow, controls the closure, and states the comparison for the rest.

## 2. The solver profile

**Rule 1.** The scenario's `[circuit]` table (RFC 012 rule 6) is the solver profile of DATA-001: the only place a learner tunes the solver (NUM-005: "the display rate is never the step"). Every field is optional and has the default below; an unknown key is `circuit.profile-unknown`; a value of the wrong type or outside its range is `circuit.profile-value` naming the expectation. Time fields are whole numbers of ticks (RFC 004 rule 25); a field marked quantity is written with its unit (DATA-010), a field marked number without.

**Rule 2.** The fields:

| Field | Type | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `method` | `"trapezoidal"` or `"gear2"` | `"trapezoidal"` | The integration method of rule 6 |
| `step` | time, at least 1 tick | `1us`, or the tick if larger | The first step, the step after every breakpoint, and under `fixed` the only step |
| `control` | `"fixed"` or `"adaptive"` | `"adaptive"` | Rule 10 |
| `min-step` | time, at least 1 tick and at most `step` | the tick | The smallest step the controller may take (rule 13) |
| `max-step` | time, at least `step` and `2 × min-step` | `100 × step` | The largest |
| `ltol` | number in `(0, 1]` | `1e-3` | The relative local truncation tolerance of rule 11 |
| `newton.max-iterations` | integer `1..=1000` | `50` | Rule 8 |
| `newton.abstol` | quantity (A), above zero | `1pA` | The absolute current term of rules 8 and 11 |
| `newton.reltol` | number in `(0, 1)` | `1e-6` | The relative term of rule 8 |
| `newton.vntol` | quantity (V), above zero | `1nV` | The absolute voltage term of rules 8 and 11 |
| `gmin` | quantity (S), from zero to `1mS` | `1pS` | The conductance from every node to ground that keeps the matrix regular (rule 4); `gmin` stepping (rule 9) starts at `1mS` |
| `max-rejections` | integer `1..=1000` | `60` | The most consecutive rejected attempts of one step before non-convergence (rule 12) |
| `on-non-convergence` | `"stop-run"` or `"hold"` | `"stop-run"` | Rule 17 |
| `faulted-policy` | `"drop"` or `"stop-run"` | `"drop"` | RFC 012 rule 17 |
| `detect-interval` | time, at least 1 tick | `max-step` | The longest gap between two advances when the scenario has a detector (RFC 012 rule 8) |
| `max-breakpoints` | integer, `1` to the Host's maximum (rule 18) | `65536` | The most pending breakpoints (RFC 012 rule 9); more ends the run with `run.limit-exceeded` |
| `max-unknowns` | integer, `1` to the Host's maximum (rule 18) | `256` | The bound of rule 18 (NUM-003); dense factorisation is cubic, so the default keeps a learner's run responsive |
| `tolerance.atol` | number, zero or more | `1e-9` | Rule 20: the absolute tolerance, a bare number in the SI unit of the value compared (RFC 004 rule 28: the tolerance applies to values of several dimensions, so no one unit could be written) |
| `tolerance.rtol` | number in `[0, 1)` | `1e-6` | Rule 20: the relative tolerance |

A value outside its range, or a combination that breaks `min-step ≤ step ≤ max-step` or `max-step ≥ 2 × min-step`, is `circuit.profile-value` naming the field. The profile is part of the scenario and so of the run identity (RFC 004: the canonical form hashes every table); two runs with different profiles are different runs.

## 3. Formulation

**Rule 3.** The unknowns `x` of modified nodal analysis (PHY-003), in a fixed order that depends on identities only (RFC 001 rule 16): the voltage of every `net` but the ground, in ascending connection id; then the current of every element whose current is an unknown (`voltage-source`, `inductor`, `ammeter`, the `dc-motor`'s armature), in ascending actor id; then every extra state an element declares (the `dc-motor`'s speed), in ascending actor id. A guest device contributes no unknown: its pins are on nets and its currents are stamped (rule 5).

**Rule 4.** The equations: Kirchhoff's current law at every non-ground net, the branch equation of every current unknown, and the state equation of every extra state. A conductance `gmin` from every net to ground is always present so that a floating net has a solution; it is in the profile because a learner who builds a floating subcircuit sees `gmin`'s effect and can turn it down to see the trouble.

**Rule 5.** The device interface, the same for a built-in in Host code and for the guest of the `device` world (RFC 012 rule 19): given the trial voltages of its pins (and, for an element with an unknown current or state, the trial values of those), a device returns the currents into its pins and the Jacobian of those currents with respect to the trial values, each in SI units, as the stamps the solver adds into the matrix and the right-hand side. A device stamps nothing but its own rows and columns (ARCH-006 within the domain: the matrix is the solver's). **Order**: in every evaluation the solver visits the devices in ascending actor identity, built-ins and the guest device alike, and adds each device's stamps in ascending port identity; the order fixes the float sums (rule 19). A reactive built-in asks the solver for the coefficients of its companion model (rule 6). A guest device receives `h` in `begin-step` (`0.0` for the initial solve) and, in 0.4, is **memoryless apart from its trial state** (a diode, a transistor without charge storage); a reactive guest device needs the method and its coefficients in `begin-step` and is a later contract version. Besides the currents and the Jacobian a device answers whether it **limited** a junction in this evaluation (rule 8): a built-in as a flag in Host code, a guest as the last entry of `evaluate`'s result (RFC 012 rule 19).

## 4. Integration

**Rule 6.** Two methods (PHY-005), chosen by the profile: **trapezoidal** (second order, A-stable, the default, because learners' RC and motor plants are smooth) and **Gear's second-order backward differentiation** (`gear2`, stiffly stable, for switching plants where trapezoidal rings). A third, **backward Euler**, is used only for starting (rule 7). For every dynamic state variable `x` of an element (a capacitor's voltage, an inductor's current, the motor's armature current and speed) the solver keeps the accepted points `(t_k, x_k)` and the derivative `x'_n` at the last one, and the element's equation uses

`x'_{n+1} = α·x_{n+1} − history`,

with, for a step `h = t_{n+1} − t_n` in seconds and `ω = h / h_n` the ratio to the previous step:

| Method | α | history |
|---|---|---|
| Backward Euler | `1/h` | `x_n / h` |
| Trapezoidal | `2/h` | `2·x_n/h + x'_n` |
| Gear 2, variable step | `(1 + 2ω) / ((1 + ω)·h)` | `((1 + ω)·x_n − (ω²/(1 + ω))·x_{n−1}) / h` |

At constant step Gear 2 reduces to `α = 3/(2h)`, `history = (4·x_n − x_{n−1})/(2h)`. Both order-2 forms were checked on an RC step with the start-up of rule 7: halving a fixed step divides the error at 2·RC by 3.89 to 3.97 (trapezoidal) and 4.01 to 4.11 (Gear 2), and by 3.87 to 3.96 and 4.01 to 4.12 with a prescribed pattern of step ratios between 0.5 and 2 (the figures approach 4 as the step falls); the coefficients are exact on quadratics at `ω` = ½, 1, 3/2 and 2. The adaptive controller never lets `ω` exceed 2, inside the zero-stability limit `1 + √2` of variable-step Gear 2. The history is state of the domain (RFC 012 rule 29).

**Rule 7.** Starting and restarting. The history is cut at tick 0 and at every applied breakpoint (RFC 012 rule 9), because the inputs are discontinuous there. After a cut: steps 1 and 2 are backward Euler of size `min(step, distance to the next breakpoint)` with no error test (their error is the learner's choice of `step`, which is why the default is small and the profile exposes it; `ltol` does not bound it: an RC of 10 µs sampled every microsecond at the default profile ends 7.7 times `ltol·V` from the analytic solution in the model of rule 21); step 3 is backward Euler with the error test of rule 11 for order 1; from step 4 the profile's method is used with the test for order 2. Backward Euler needs no derivative, and it satisfies the element equations at its end point, so the derivative `x'` it leaves is the exact right-hand side. The first step of a run follows the initial solve (rule 9). Under `fixed` there is no error test (rule 10): steps 1 and 2 are backward Euler and every later step uses the profile's method.

## 5. Nonlinear iteration

**Rule 8.** Newton–Raphson (PHY-004) at every attempted step. The start is the previous accepted solution. Each iteration visits the devices in the order of rule 5, with the trial values of the unknowns; a guest device is called with `evaluate` (RFC 012 rule 20) and its result checked before it is used; the solver assembles `J` and `f`, solves `J·dx = −f` (rule 22), and forms `x + dx`. **The solver changes no trial value: a junction is limited inside the device that has it**, as SPICE3 does, because only the device knows its junctions, its `is` and its `n`, and moving a node voltage to limit one junction is undefined where nets are shared. The built-in diode keeps the junction voltage it used at the previous iteration of the step, limits the new one with SPICE3's `pnjlim(v_new, v_old)` (`vte = n·VT`, `VT = 0.025852` V, a literal so that every Host has the same bits; `vcrit = vte·ln(vte / (√2·is))`): when `v_new > vcrit` and `|v_new − v_old| > 2·vte`, if `v_old > 0` then with `a = (v_new − v_old)/vte` the result is `v_old + vte·(2 + ln(a − 2))` for `a > 0` and `v_old − vte·(2 + ln(2 − a))` otherwise, and if `v_old ≤ 0` it is `vte·ln(v_new/vte)`, the device reporting that it limited; otherwise `v_new` stands (the form of `DEVpnjlim` in SPICE3f5 and ngspice, not the older `1 + a` form that gives 11 iterations in the figure below); it evaluates its characteristic at the limited voltage, returns the current and the conductance there extended linearly to the trial voltage (`i(v_lim) + g(v_lim)·(v − v_lim)` and `g(v_lim)`), and reports that it limited. A guest device may do the same with the `Trial` helper of its SDK, which carries `pnjlim` with the same literals, and reports it in the last entry of `evaluate`'s result; a guest that does not limit returns its model at the trial it was given. A diode's characteristic is `is·(exp(v/vte) − 1) + gmin·v` and continues linearly, with the slope at `v/vte = 80`, beyond that point so that `exp` never overflows. **Converged** when all of: every unknown's update satisfies `|dx_i| ≤ reltol·max(|x_i|, |x_i + dx_i|) + tol_i`, with `tol_i = vntol` for a voltage or the motor's speed and `abstol` for a current; every node's residual satisfies `|f_i| ≤ abstol + reltol·s_i`, `s_i` the sum of the magnitudes of the branch currents at that node at the trial (so that large conductances such as a switch's `ron` do not defeat an absolute test); and no device reported limiting in the last iteration. More than `newton.max-iterations` iterations is a convergence failure (rule 16). The final `x + dx` is the solution; `accept-step` is handed its pin voltages. A scalar model of a diode and a 1 kohm resistor stepped from 0 V to 5 V needs 59 iterations without the limiter, more than the default bound of 50, and 8 with it, by the criteria of this rule and SPICE3's `pnjlim`.

**Rule 9.** The initial solve, in the start hook (RFC 012 rule 30), after the held values of the `constant`s and the outputs of the comparators are set and the controllers' initial outputs recorded, so that the sources and signals in force at tick 0 are known. The dynamic states are taken as follows. A capacitor or an inductor **with an `ic`** is replaced by a voltage source (capacitor) or a current source (inductor) of that value, so that its state is the learner's; one **without** is taken in its DC form, a capacitor open (only the node-to-ground `gmin` of rule 4 at its nodes) and an inductor short, so that its state is what the sources give at tick 0; the `dc-motor` is taken in its DC steady state (`di/dt = 0`, `dω/dt = 0`). The algebraic unknowns are solved by Newton from zero (rule 8). A guest device is called in DC form: `begin-step(0, 0.0)`, `evaluate` per iteration, `accept-step(solution)`. **Stages**: the plain solve; on failure **`gmin` stepping**, the same solve with `gmin` at `1mS` and then at each decade below while it stays above both the profile's `gmin` and `1e-15 S`, the last level being the profile's `gmin` itself (zero included), each level starting from the previous solution; on failure **source stepping**, every independent source (a `voltage-source`'s `v + gain·cmd` and a `current-source`'s `i + gain·cmd`) scaled by `λ = 0.1, 0.2, … 1` in ten levels, each starting from the previous; the Newton iteration of every solve is bounded by `newton.max-iterations`. A guest device's calls in one such solve are one compound activation, keyed as the device's own `init` event (RFC 012 rule 21); every solve ends with `reject-step` except the one that gives the result, which ends with `accept-step`. A singular matrix (a zero pivot: a circuit with no direct-current operating point until an `ic` is given, such as a capacitor with an `ic` directly across a voltage source, an inductor without an `ic` across a voltage source, or two inductors without an `ic` in parallel) or the failure of all three stages refuses the run at start with `circuit.no-operating-point`, naming the unknown of the first zero pivot or of the largest residual and saying whether it is a voltage, a current or a state: a validation of the plant a learner can act on (UI-009). A node with no direct-current path to ground is not refused but warned about at validation (`circuit.floating-node`; the elements that conduct direct current between their pins are the `resistor`, the `voltage-source`, an `inductor` without an `ic` (a short), the `ammeter`, the `diode`, the `switch`, the `dc-motor`'s armature, a `capacitor` with an `ic` (a voltage source) and the pins of a guest device, and those that do not are the `capacitor` without an `ic`, the `current-source`, an `inductor` with an `ic` (a current source), the `voltmeter` and the `comparator`): `gmin` gives it the voltage its injected current produces across it, which a current source into a capacitor without an `ic` makes enormous, and the warning says why. The result is the state at tick 0, with which `solved` starts.

## 6. Step control

**Rule 10.** Two policies (PHY-010), applied inside an advance (RFC 012 rule 8): **fixed**, every step is `step` ticks, shortened only to end at a limit (a breakpoint or the advance's tick), with no error test (rule 7), and a convergence failure is a non-convergence at once (rule 17); **adaptive**, the default, in which the step controller chooses each step by rules 11 to 13. The controller's proposal for the next step is kept apart from the step actually taken: a step shortened to end at a limit does not shrink the proposal.

**Rule 11.** The local truncation error estimate, over the **dynamic state variables** of rule 6 only (the algebraic unknowns jump at a breakpoint and say nothing about the integration). With the divided differences `dd2` and `dd3` of the state over the last three or four accepted points since the last cut **including the candidate point** (they are defined for unequal spacing, which is what a variable step needs):

| Step | Estimate for each state variable |
|---|---|
| Backward Euler, order 1 | `h² · abs(dd2)` |
| Trapezoidal, order 2 | `6·(1/12)·h³·abs(dd3) = h³·abs(dd3) / 2` |
| Gear 2, order 2 | `(1 + ω)² / (ω·(1 + 2ω)) · h³ · abs(dd3)`, `ω = h / h_n` |

(`1/12` is the error constant of the trapezoidal rule and `C(ω) = (1 + ω)² / (6ω(1 + 2ω))` that of variable-step Gear 2, `2/9` at `ω = 1`, `3/8` at `ω = ½`, `3/20` at `ω = 2`; the estimate is `6·C·h³·abs(dd3)` because `dd3 ≈ x‴/6`, and the constant-step `2/9` would read up to three times low after a rejection. A predictor–corrector difference would need other factors, `1/6`, `1/13`, `2/11` depending on the pair, and the first text's `1/3` matches none.) The **error ratio** `r` is the largest over the state variables of `estimate / (ltol·max(|x|, floor) + tol)`, where `floor` and `tol` are `vntol` for a voltage or a speed and `abstol` for a current. A plant with no dynamic element has no estimate and its step grows to `max-step`.

**Rule 12.** Accept and reject. An attempt that converged with `r ≤ 1` is **accepted**: for the guest device `accept-step(solution)` commits (RFC 012 rule 20), the history takes the point, and the proposal for the next step is `floor(n·f)` ticks, `n` the ticks of the step just taken and `f = min(2, 0.9·r^e)` with `e = −1/2` for order 1 and `−1/3` for order 2 (`1/sqrt(r)` and `1/cbrt(r)`, from `libm` where not exact; `r = 0` gives `f = 2`, and `f` is at least 0.9 because `r ≤ 1`), computed in f64 and rounded down, then clamped to `[min-step, max-step]`; after a step **shortened** to end at a limit the proposal is the larger of this and the previous proposal (rule 10). Under Gear 2 the next step is at most twice the step just taken (`ω ≤ 2`, inside the zero-stability limit `1 + √2`). An attempt with `r > 1` or a convergence failure is **rejected**: the guest device's `reject-step` discards (RFC 012 rule 21), and the step is attempted again from the same start with `floor(n·clamp(0.9·r^e, 0.25, 0.9))` ticks (`floor(n/4)` for a convergence failure), at least `min-step`. At `n = min-step` the two causes part: a failed **error test** is accepted anyway, since nothing smaller is allowed, and notified as `circuit.step-minimum`; a **convergence** failure is non-convergence (rule 17). More than `max-rejections` consecutive rejections of one step is non-convergence. Rejection is therefore bounded: at most `max-rejections` attempts per step, each at most `newton.max-iterations` evaluations of the guest device, and each attempt one compound activation under **one budget** of the device class (RFC 003 rule 12); a scenario lowers the class's `budget` in its limits, and the kit's hostile scenario does so.

**Rule 13.** Bounds, in ticks. A step is a whole number of ticks from `min-step` to `max-step` (the profile requires `max-step ≥ 2 × min-step`), except where it ends at a limit. With `d` the ticks from the step's start to the nearest limit (the next breakpoint or the advance's tick) and `n` the step the controller proposes: if `n ≥ d` the step is `d` and ends at the limit; otherwise, if the remainder `d − n` is below `min-step`, the step is `d − min-step` when `d ≥ 2 × min-step`, so that the last step before the limit is exactly `min-step`, and `d` when it is not; otherwise the step is `n`. A step is below `min-step` only when `d` is, the one case in which a step is: it is the whole remaining distance, and nothing is notified.

**Rule 14.** Time in the arithmetic: a step of `n` ticks is `h = n · tick` seconds, where the tick length is the scenario's exact decimal converted to the nearest f64 once at composition; `now` is never converted (the solver never needs absolute time, only `h`, the ratio of two steps and the history). This keeps every step's coefficients identical across Hosts and keeps accumulated rounding out of the plant: the ticks accumulate, not the seconds.

**Rule 15.** Detecting a condition (RFC 012 rule 13). After every accepted step of an advance, for each detector, the solver evaluates `d = v_p − v_n` and the output the detector's hysteresis gives from the output it had at the start of the step. No step is rejected for a crossing and there is no bisection: a crossing is a fact about the end points of an accepted step. The **located crossing** of a change is the tick `s + ceil(φ·n)`, with `s` the step's start tick, `n` its length in ticks and `φ = clamp((θ − d_prev) / (d_cur − d_prev), 0, 1)`, `θ` the threshold the change crossed (`threshold + hysteresis/2` rising, `threshold − hysteresis/2` falling), computed in f64; `φ = 0` when `d_cur = d_prev` and when the condition already held at the start of the step, as at the first step of a run or after a breakpoint that moved the threshold. When an advance holds several changes the located crossing reported is that of the last, the one that left the output different from the one it had at the start of the advance; chatter that leaves it equal publishes nothing (RFC 012 rule 13). It is reported as data on the `condition` channel; the event itself is scheduled by the advance's handler (RFC 012 rule 8) on the microstep after the advance.

## 7. Failure

**Rule 16.** A convergence failure is Newton reaching `newton.max-iterations` without rule 8's criteria. A device returning a non-finite stamp, a stamp of the wrong length or a limited flag that is neither 0 nor 1 is the device's contract violation `circuit.stamp-invalid` (RFC 012 rule 20): the device faults, the step is discarded, and the attempt is made again without the device, which is absent from then on (RFC 012 rule 17). Under `fixed` a convergence failure is a non-convergence at once.

**Rule 17.** Non-convergence is a convergence failure at `n = min-step`, or more than `max-rejections` consecutive rejections of one step, or a convergence failure under `fixed`. The domain notifies `circuit.non-convergence` (`actor: name` of the device with the largest residual, `tick: tick`, `iterations: count`) and acts by the profile: `stop-run` ends the run through the domain's end-run action with the end reason `non-convergence` (RFC 012 finding F-7), the state being that of the last accepted step, which is the actionable state of UI-009; `hold` freezes the plant at the last accepted step: from then on an advance only makes the sensors sample the frozen state, the plant's inputs are no longer recorded as breakpoints and the pending ones are dropped (RFC 012 rule 9), the signals keep their held values, every controller keeps sampling, and the code is notified once. Observation short of failure: an advance in which attempts were rejected notifies one `circuit.step-rejected` (info; `tick` of the advance, `attempts: count`, `cause`: `error` or `convergence`, that of the first), and an accepted step whose error test failed at `min-step` is `circuit.step-minimum` (warning). These are **notifications** of the domain (RFC 008 rule 3) for the diagnostic log and the timeline, never guest inputs and never queue events: they take no sequence number, so how many attempts a Host made cannot change any key of the trace (RFC 001 rule 39). The world inspection (RFC 012 finding F-12) answers the current proposal, the method, the steps, iterations and rejections of the last advance and the plant's status; it is exported with the domain's state (RFC 012 rule 29).

## 8. Size and memory

**Rule 18.** The estimate (NUM-003), made by the domain's `validate` before anything is instantiated (the admission estimate counts guest instances and memory only): with `n` unknowns of rule 3 the solver needs `8·n²` bytes for the dense matrix of rule 22, `8·6n` for the vectors and history, `32 × max-breakpoints` for the pending breakpoints at their bound (RFC 012 rule 9), and the guest device's own memory is the module's (RFC 003); `n > max-unknowns` is refused at scenario load with `circuit.too-large` naming `n`, the bound and the bytes (RFC 012 rule 28). The default bound of 256 unknowns is 0.5 MiB of matrix and about ten milliseconds per factorisation; it is a profile field that a scenario can lower and can raise up to the Host's maximum, so that a larger plant on the native Host is one line. **Host maxima** (RFC 001 rule 13: a scenario may lower a limit and cannot raise it above the Host's maximum; the form of RFC 003 rule 19), which the Host gives the domain when it builds it:

| Name | Default | Host maximum, browser | Host maximum, native |
|---|---|---|---|
| `max-unknowns` | 256 | 512 (2 MiB of matrix) | 1,024 (8 MiB) |
| `max-breakpoints` | 65,536 | 262,144 (8 MiB) | 1,000,000 (32 MB) |

The maxima are starting figures: the unit that writes the solver measures one factorisation at each maximum on every engine and records the time and the memory with the figures of a release; the measurement may lower them, and only an amendment raises them. A profile value above the Host's maximum is `circuit.profile-value`.

## 9. Numbers across Hosts

**Rule 19.** Arithmetic rules, so that two Hosts compute the same bits wherever the engines allow (NUM-001, NUM-002; RFC 001 rules 56 to 63), **and the controls that keep them so** (the owner's decision D3: what the tolerance does not cover is controlled). The solver uses `f64` with IEEE 754 semantics only; no fused multiply-add (the crate never calls `mul_add`; Rust emits none otherwise; WebAssembly has no scalar fused operation); no SIMD (NUM-001 keeps it to the domain's tier, and the tier is "within tolerance", so none is taken); every reduction runs in the fixed order of rule 5; every function that is not exactly rounded by IEEE comes from one pure-Rust implementation compiled into both Hosts, never from the platform's math library (RFC 001 rule 60): the `libm` crate (licensed MIT, allowed by RFC 006 rule 6), pinned exactly, for `exp`, `ln`, `cbrt` and any other; the operations taken from `std` are only `+ − × ÷`, negation, `sqrt`, `abs`, `floor`, `ceil`, `trunc`, `copysign`, comparisons and casts (the crate has its own `fmin` and `fmax`, by comparison, because `f64::min` and `f64::max` leave the result for a pair of signed zeros to the target); the LU of rule 22 pivots by the largest magnitude with the lowest index on ties, a rule that depends on values only; every f64 that leaves the solver (a held signal, a report, a sent output, a checkpoint) is made canonical (RFC 001 rule 59); a NaN anywhere in the solution is a convergence failure (rule 16), never a result. The controls:

1. `crates/florn-domain-circuit/clippy.toml` lists as `disallowed-methods` every `std` float method outside the list above (at least `exp`, `exp2`, `exp_m1`, `ln`, `ln_1p`, `log`, `log2`, `log10`, `powf`, `powi`, `cbrt`, `hypot`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan`, `atan2`, `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh`, `mul_add`, `min`, `max`), so that `cargo clippy -D warnings` (the routine and CI) fails on their use.
2. `ci/check-deps.py` allows the crate the direct dependencies of the core crates it needs and `libm`, and nothing else.
3. No target-feature or code-generation flag changes float behaviour anywhere in the workspace: a script in `ci/` fails on `target-cpu`, `target-feature`, `-Z` options or fast-math flags in `.cargo/config*`, `RUSTFLAGS` of the workflows, `rust-toolchain.toml` or `Cargo.toml` profiles.
4. A kit test, `bit_identity`, runs a fixed table of inputs (every `libm` function the solver calls, signed zeros, infinities, subnormals, NaN payloads) through the domain's arithmetic and compares the bits exactly, natively on three architectures and in three browsers; it fails when the crate calls a `libm` function the table does not list, and its table is printed with the figures of a release.

Under these rules the kit's figures are expected to show **bit-identical results** on x86-64 and AArch64 natively and in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, as the guests' arithmetic does for everything but the sign of a default NaN (RFC 003 section 9), which rule 59 of RFC 001 removes at the contract. Tier 2 nevertheless promises rule 20, not identity: the promise is what the project can hold if an engine's `f64` differs in a corner the kit does not cover.

**Rule 20.** The comparison of Tier 2 (RFC 001 rule 62; PHY-012; the owner's decision D3). It is the cross-host comparison of a scenario that declares the circuit domain, a continuous class (RFC 012 rule 1): it applies when the two runs are of one florn version and differ in Host, engine or architecture (RFC 001 rules 41 and 62, Tier 2). Two runs of one build are Tier 1 and are compared exactly, by digest, where any difference is `diverged`; runs of two florn versions are Tier 3, which promises nothing before 1.0.0 (RFC 001 section 1.1), and a difference is `diverged` there too; the scenarios of discrete domains are exact under Tier 2 as well. The trace holds BLAKE3 digests of payloads, and domain events that call no actor leave no record (RFC 005 section 5), so the plant reaches the trace through the numeric payloads of the activations it causes, and the comparison needs **both runs' trace records and payload bytes**. Records are paired by position, the n-th of one run with the n-th of the other, and a pair **agrees** when all of:

1. kind, key (tick, microstep, class, tie value, origin, sequence number) and target are equal, and the effects have the same structure (kinds, sequence numbers, ports and channels, payload lengths);
2. each **numeric field** the pair holds satisfies `|a − b| ≤ atol + rtol·max(|a|, |b|)` in SI units with the profile's `tolerance`, a field held in another unit being converted by that unit's factor first (a `probe` or a `send` is in its signal's unit, a `control` field in the module's `control-units`), every NaN being one value (RFC 003 open question 4) and two infinities of the same sign equal. The numeric fields are the `f64` fields of a report on a channel whose layout the domain or the module declares (RFC 008 rule 17: `probe`'s value, `control`'s three, `condition`'s output) and the eight bytes of a `send` on a `signal-out` port (a controller's or a sensor's output, RFC 012 rule 11). Every other field, a tick such as `condition`'s `located`, a timer's delay, a count, is compared exactly;
3. every other payload, a report on a channel with no declared layout included, is equal by digest;
4. the consumed work and the number of random draws of an activation of a continuous-class actor (a controller, the guest device, a sensor, a detector) are **exempt** from the comparison: a branch of the guest's code that a value within tolerance flips changes them. They are still compared under Tier 1, and still bounded by the budget, which faults an activation that exceeds it; the kit records them and reports differences without failing on them, so that the exemption rests on evidence.

The outcome is **`matched`** when every stored chain point is equal; **`within-tolerance`** when the chains differ, the two traces hold the same number of records and every pair agrees, reported by `replay.within-tolerance`; **`diverged`** otherwise (the counts differ, or a pair does not agree), naming the first record that differs by `replay.value-differs` (a numeric value outside the tolerance, with both values) or `replay.record-differs` (its key, its structure, a payload, or the length); **`needs-payloads`** when the chains differ and the trace records or the payload bytes of either run are missing, saying which by `replay.needs-payloads` (a run record exported without `--trace --payloads`; the preview of RFC 005 rule 30 says so): a payload-less record whose chains agree is `matched`, as it is expected to be. The kernel's own `Comparison::Incomparable`, stored points at different positions, is unchanged and still reported as `replay.diverged`. The runner's `replay` and the Host's comparison return and print the outcome; `matched` and `within-tolerance` are exit code 0 with the word printed, `diverged` is 2 as today, `needs-payloads` is 1. The outcome is a result of the replay, not a field of the record: formats stay frozen. A run's digest is still the digest of its trace: two runs that are `within-tolerance` have different digests. **A plant with a guest device** leaves one activation record per accepted step, so two runs whose step sequences differ hold different numbers of records and are `diverged`; for such a plant the comparison is `within-tolerance` only while the step sequences are the same, which bit identity (rule 19) is there to keep.

**Rule 21.** Goldens (AUTO-004). For each circuit scenario of RFC 012 rule 27 the kit records, natively on x86-64, a **results file** `scenarios/results/<name>.results`: flat text with the first line `florn-results 1` (the version, checked by the format test), then one line per trace record, carrying everything rule 20 reads: the record's kind, its whole key and its target; its effects' kinds, sequence numbers, ports or channels and payload lengths; for each numeric field the bits of the `f64` in hex; for each other payload its digest; and, recorded and reported but not compared for a continuous-class actor, the consumed work and the draws. A kit command writes the file from a run record exported with payloads, so that the browser scripts and the native comparison read the same text without a TOML parser; its syntax is frozen by the kit's format test, and the digest of the scenario stays in `digests.toml` as before. Every other Host and engine is compared to the file by rule 20, as a second run. Two analytic goldens come besides. **`rc-step`**: a 1 kohm resistor and a 1 uF capacitor with `ic = 0V` (RC = 1 ms) on a `voltage-source` of 1 V from tick 0 with a scheduled change to 0 V at 2 ms (a restart), tick 1 ns, a `voltmeter` across the capacitor with period 100 us, end 5 ms, the default profile: the voltmeter's samples against the analytic solution (`V·(1 − exp(−t/RC))` to 2 ms, then the decay from the value there) at every sample, within **`4·ltol·V`** under either method. The model of rules 6 to 13 with whole ticks (run 2026-10-31) measures 0.26 and 0.83 times `ltol·V` for trapezoidal and Gear 2, so the bound is a regression guard with a margin; it holds for a plant whose time constant is many `step`s (rule 7), and the golden claims no more. **`guest-diode` against `diode-clip`**: the same plant with the guest device in the built-in's place; the two are two scenarios, so this is a test of the kit and not rule 20: the two voltmeters' samples, paired by position, agree within the profile's tolerance, which is the test that a guest device and a built-in are the same thing to the solver.

## 10. Linear algebra

**Rule 22.** In 0.4 the solver factors a dense matrix by LU with partial pivoting, written in the crate (about two hundred lines), because the plants of Phase 4 have tens of unknowns and a dense factorisation is the most deterministic thing available: no reordering heuristics, no fill-in decisions, one pivot rule (rule 19). PHY-006's sparse storage and solving is **deferred** to the release that first needs a plant above the bound of rule 18 (open question 1); when it comes, the sparse library is chosen by a measurement against this dense baseline on the kit's largest scenario, and must be pure Rust under RFC 006 rule 6 (and REQ PHY-006) with a pivot rule that depends on values only, or the domain keeps its own.

## 11. Verification

| Property | Test |
|---|---|
| Formulation | `rc-step`: the transient matches the analytic solution within rule 21's bound under trapezoidal and under Gear 2, at the default profile and with `max-step = RC/10` |
| Order | `rc-step` without its scheduled change, with `control = "fixed"`, a voltmeter period of 1 ms (no step is shortened) and `step` = RC/8, RC/16, RC/32, RC/64: the error at the sample at 2 ms falls by a factor between 3.8 and 4.2 at every halving under both methods (measured 3.89 to 3.97 and 4.01 to 4.11); a unit test of the integrator with a prescribed step pattern of ratios from 0.5 to 2 under Gear 2 gives the same (measured 4.01 to 4.12) |
| Start-up | After a cut the first two steps are backward Euler of the profile's `step` and the third is backward Euler with the order-1 test; the scheduled change of `rc-step` does not push the error above rule 21's bound |
| Newton | `diode-clip` converges at every step within `max-iterations`; a diode stepped from 0 V to 5 V converges with the limiter (8 iterations in the scalar model) and a unit test with the limiter off needs more than the default bound (59); the built-in diode and `guest-diode` with the SDK's limiter agree within tolerance; a device that limited does not converge in that iteration; the iteration count of an advance is in the world inspection |
| Step control | `motor-loop` under `adaptive` takes fewer steps than under `fixed` at `min-step`, with the same results within tolerance; each advance with a rejected attempt notifies one `circuit.step-rejected` counting them; no step is below `min-step` except the whole distance to a limit (rule 13); an error test that fails at `min-step` is accepted and notified as `circuit.step-minimum` |
| Breakpoints | A change at tick T acts from the step that starts at T (RFC 012 rule 9); the history is cut there and the next three steps are backward Euler |
| Failure | `hostile-diverge` (a `bad-device` whose stamps never converge) ends with `non-convergence` under `stop-run` and continues frozen under `hold`, controllers still sampling; the attempts per step never exceed `max-rejections` |
| Initial solve | A plant that needs `gmin` stepping and one that needs source stepping start; a plant with no solution, a capacitor with an `ic` directly across a voltage source and an inductor without an `ic` across one are refused with `circuit.no-operating-point` before the first event; a current source into a capacitor without an `ic` warns `circuit.floating-node`; a capacitor without an `ic` across a voltage source starts at the source's voltage, and an inductor without an `ic` fed by a current source starts at that current; a guest device's initial-solve calls leave one record, keyed as its `init` |
| Estimate | A scenario above `max-unknowns` is refused before anything is instantiated, naming the bytes; a profile value above the Host's maximum, in the browser and natively, is `circuit.profile-value` |
| Controls | `cargo clippy` fails on a `std` `exp` or `f64::min` placed in the crate; `ci/check-deps.py` fails on a second dependency; the flag script fails on a `target-cpu` in a scratch copy; `bit_identity` fails on a `libm` call missing from its table; each is a test of the control, not only of the code |
| Bits | `bit_identity` and every circuit scenario's results are compared across x86-64, AArch64, Chromium, Firefox and WebKit by rule 20: the kit reports `matched` or `within-tolerance` per pair and the release notes print the table; the expectation of rule 19 is a figure, not a promise |
| Comparison | A run compared with itself is `matched`; its payload-less record is `matched` where the chains agree and, across Hosts and doctored so that a chain differs, `needs-payloads` (on one build, `diverged`); a doctored last-bit difference in one report, and a difference in consumed work alone, are `within-tolerance` across Hosts and `diverged` on one build; a numeric difference beyond the tolerance, a different `condition` tick and a different record count are `diverged`, each with its `replay` code |
| Checkpoint | Restored at its half, every circuit scenario continues `matched` to the uninterrupted run under the same Host and `within-tolerance` or `matched` under the other |
| Determinism of the kernel part | The keys and targets of every record of every circuit scenario are byte-identical on every engine (Tier 1 for the discrete part of a continuous domain) |

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Reason for rejection |
|---|---|
| An external solver crate (nalgebra, faer, sprs) in 0.4 | Each adds a dependency tree under RFC 006 rule 6 for plants of tens of unknowns; none promises a value-only pivot rule across versions. The dense LU is small and auditable; the sparse question is deferred with a measurement attached |
| A predictor–corrector error estimate with a linear predictor | Its error is O(h²) and cannot estimate an O(h³) local error; the divided differences of the accepted points need no predictor and handle unequal steps |
| Local error over every unknown | The algebraic unknowns jump at a breakpoint and would drive the step to its minimum after every controller output |
| Higher-order Gear (up to 6) | Stability and step-ratio handling cost more than the lab gains; `gear2` covers the switching plants |
| Bisection to locate a crossing | The crossing cannot be scheduled into the past (RFC 001 rule 5); the located time is reported as data instead |
| A junction limiter in the solver, for every device | The solver cannot know a guest's junctions, `is` or `n`, and moving node voltages to limit one junction is undefined where nets are shared; the limiter lives in the device and a guest reports that it limited |
| `solver` events in the queue | They take sequence numbers, so the number of attempts a Host made would shift every later key of the trace: observation changing the trace (RFC 001 rule 39). Rejections and non-convergence are notifications |
| A single fixed step and no error control | PHY-010 asks for both; and a fixed step is what a learner sets when the adaptive one surprises them, so both exist |
| Comparing the whole trace within tolerance | A tolerance on integers is meaningless and hides a real divergence; ticks, classes, targets and structure stay exact and only numeric payloads take the tolerance |
| Streaming results as f32 to save space | The results are the domain's promise; they stay f64 in the trace. The display path (RFC 014) may use f32 because it is not compared |
| Treating a NaN result as a value to compare | A NaN is a failure of the solver, not a result; rule 16 catches it before it can be one |
